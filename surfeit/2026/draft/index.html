The Lowdown

by the Commissioner

Every Draft Is a List of Assumptions

Every draft is a planning document. Ten of us just published our assumptions about the 2026 season, in writing, with timestamps, and the season’s entire job is to red-team them. Welcome to The Surfeit! This is the closest many of us will come to a real wargame; may your assumptions be validated!

Three assumption classes showed up on draft night. First, concentration bets: L’entente Discordiale holds three 49ers and three Bills, which means two NFL games a week decide most of their fate. Jordan James at pick 123, against a consensus rank of 254, is the same bet with the afterburner lit. Second, divestment bets: Jesse (Swanson) and SHAC drafted zero kickers. Divest-to-invest is real doctrine. This is divest-and-hope, and I respect the commitment. Third, timing bets, and I’ll go first because I made the most obvious one: I punted TE until round 14 while the league median said round 7, and my reward was Juwan Johnson. Swifty Mahomey at least got paid for waiting, landing the actual Patrick Mahomes at pick 114 after wearing his name all August.

Still, assumption-heavy plans win wars all the time. The waiver wire is our defense industrial base; half of these teams will get bailed out by October and we’ll all pretend the plan worked.

The balloon goes up next Thursday. The full board, with every pick’s delta against consensus rank, is on the draft page now, along with standings and team pages. Best of luck to all nine of you, and slightly less to France and the UK, who play me week one. Matchup overview to follow next week!

Draft Power Rankings

Draft Power Rankings: Assessing Ten Unique Force Designs

Every draft is a force design, and this is your design debrief. All models are wrong, but some are useful. I’ll describe the method first: all 150 picks measured against the FantasyPros half-PPR consensus board; a team’s score is the sum of its deltas, positive when value fell to you, negative when you bought early. The counterargument is real and conceded up front: consensus measures the market, and wars are won by the force you field, so a minus number attached to the right roster is just the cost of conviction. Ten designs, assessed in order.

1. Dave (+1)

Somehow, the sole roster to get a positive value vs. ADP. Is my methodology flawed? Who knows. Regardless, please lord this over the rest of the Skunks and wolves next week.

2. Los Bandidos (-42)

Trevor Lawrence at a 37-pick discount and Josh Jacobs late (who is now no longer on the roster). The design holds one assumption per capability area instead of five, which is why the bill stayed small. The Fairbairn kicker premium (minus-80) is the stain on the team’s positivity.

3. SHACtin a fool (-115)

Bijan Robinson plus an Atlanta triple is a concentrated bet on one offense, and Jaxson Dart 23 picks late hedges the quarterback risk for cheap. Two defenses is one more than doctrine requires, and Seattle at minus-68 was the expensive one.

4. Swanson (-137)

Two different city triples (Chicago and Denver), which is a wargame where two scenarios decide everything. Caleb Williams 29 picks late funds part of it. High variance, priced accordingly.

5. Wild SeeKats (-143)

The most conventional force structure in the league: one of everything, nothing exotic, no assumption stacked on another. Minus-143 almost entirely from paying early at kicker (Cam Little, minus-73). The design is sound; the parts procurement needs work.

6. Swifty Mahomey (-167)

Landed the actual Patrick Mahomes at pick 114 after wearing his name all August, then built a four-piece Rams block around Stafford anyway, which is hedging your identity crisis at market rates. Two stacks show real theory of victory. The Rams defense at minus-84 paid for it.

7. Gary (-189)

Jared Goff 28 picks late was clean value. Jason Myers 95 picks early is the single largest kicker premium in the league, and at this table that is a contested title. One design choice is carrying half the deficit.

8. Statistical Anomalies (-193)

My design, and the review board goes hardest here because I chair it. I assumed tight end was optional until round 14, the league median said round 7, and my reward was Juwan Johnson. Joe Burrow 16 late and a plus-16 start could have anchored something; the Dicker kicker buy at minus-80 and the tight end timing gave it all back. Eighth is generous and I will not be appealing.

9. MJ (-330)

Amon-Ra St. Brown, Sam LaPorta, and Jake Bates is all-in on Detroit as the future operating environment. If Detroit is the future, this is the best draft in the league; the minus-330 is the premium for deciding that in August, with Bates at minus-91 the steepest single line item.

10. L’entente Discordiale (-413)

The coalition committed fully: three 49ers, three Bills, and Jordan James 131 picks early with the afterburner lit. Two NFL locker rooms now carry an entire fantasy season, which is the most honest force design in the league and also the most flammable. Minus-413 is what conviction costs before the shooting starts, and the shooting starts Thursday.

One structural finding for the whole room: the steepest premiums in this draft were paid at kicker and defense, where seven of the ten teams each burned 70 picks or more of value. We are a league that pays premium rates for commodity capabilities, and False Assumptions will be checking invoices in December. Full board with every delta is on the Draft page.

Correction — ranking methodology

Added after publication. The capsules below the original ranking are unchanged; what follows corrects the arithmetic that ordered them.

The ranking above scored each team as the sum of every pick’s deviation from the consensus board, kickers and defenses included. On this board that was the wrong denominator: 83% of all the deviation in this draft came from special teams. Overall consensus ranks every kicker and defense below the draftable range while the lineup forces all ten teams to draft them, so those numbers measure the reference board’s shape, not a roster decision. Nobody chose to pay that tax and nobody could avoid it.
