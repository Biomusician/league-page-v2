Common Tactical Picture · Week 1

Wild SeeKats (Seabass/Kats) vs Los Bandidos (Bandit)

Coalition Warfare FEATURE

I regret giving the SeeKats my exact draft rankings, unedited - many snipes occurred. The combined logistics expertise of Kats combined with Seebass’ killer instinct to yield truly scary offspring. Wild SeeKats is one of two two-parent households in the league, and it shows in the tidiest team in the nursery: Jahmyr Gibbs and Breece Hall, A.J. Brown and George Pickens, and Jayden Daniels under center (if he can stay healthy). There’s a second tight end jammed into the FLEX, because Harold Fannin was the healthiest kid in the crib. Single parent Bandit is raising Trey McBride and George Kittle in one house with a single tight end slot between them - two cribs and one bedroom. He also surgically corrected the birth defect in his team and dropped Josh Jacobs. Two parents and one lineup against one parent and one too many tight ends.

Statistical Anomalies (McLovin) vs L’entente Discordiale (Borat/Jack)

Coalition Warfare MAJOR

Borat (and Jack, for like three picks) showed up with a beautiful full-term baby: Christian McCaffrey in year ten, Josh Allen, Ashton Jeanty, and Mike Evans (year 13!) riding the bench next to DK Metcalf and a second team defense nobody needs. They may have designed a better infant team. Then, see my team: one of the preemies of the litter: Jeremiyah Love and TreVeyon Henderson in the backfield, Carnell Tate and De’Zhaun Stribling on the bench, and Juwan Johnson at tight end, who is less a starter than a responsible adult I asked to babysit the rest of the team. Statistical anomalies will be required for my hopium team to triumph here.

SHACtin' a Fool 🐻 (SHAC) vs Dave

MAJOR

SHAC is a Weapons Director by trade. His literal job is looking at a picture, listening to comms, and noticing what’s missing; he took a similar approach to team construction. He drafted Lamar Jackson, Bijan Robinson, Malik Nabers and Kyle Pitts, then took two defenses (poor WRM), then took no kicker whatsoever. To this day (Friday the 4th) his K slot remains as empty as our concepts backlog. Dave (or rather, his McLovin/SHAC-aligned militia group, since he was executing a water birth) similarly missed a position (DST) but stacked depth: Xavier Worthy, Marvin Harrison and Stefon Diggs are sitting on his bench behind CeeDee Lamb and Brock Bowers. Overall, a very defensible lineup, other than the fact he dropped an 11th-round pick to grab a DST.

MJS HURT Locker (MJ) vs Swifty Mahomey (Pappie)

STANDARD

Grandpappie drafted a team that almost averages 30 years old (29.2). His infant team entered the world with a monk haircut and strong opinions about “the good old times” and gardening. Pappie is starting Travis Kelce in his 14th season and Davante Adams in his 13th, with Matthew Stafford (18th) parked on the bench in case of (extreme, hopefully-avoidable) emergency. MJ is leading a similarly ancient baby (?) team headlined by Derrick Henry in year 11 and Aaron Jones in year 10 (same draft class as Myles Garrett, Trubisky, Fournette, CMC, Mixon, James Conner, old D-Watson, and finally Mahomes, who ended up on Pappie’s team). Both had the sense to put a real receiver out front, Justin Jefferson on one side and Amon-Ra St. Brown on the other, which is the only reason either baby daycare passes inspection. Someone go get these two teams some butterscotch candies and rocking chairs.

Swanson vs Gary

STANDARD

The other baby with no kicker. ol’ Jesse (Ron?) Swanson has Puka, Rashee Rice and Chase Brown, a hands-based healthy child, and another instance of an empty K slot that has survived every lineup change he’s made, with Alvin Kamara in year ten on the bench doing nothing about it. However, Gary assembled one of the most aggressively fine, coordinated, and vocal rosters in the league: Herbert, Jonathan Taylor, Kyren Williams, DeVonta Smith, Ladd, and Tucker Kraft. Nothing wrong with any of it, unless Tucker is slow to come back from last year’s injury. Definitely nothing that would put either team in the NICU, and they’re probably both good to take their teams home from the hospital the next morning - that is, after Jesse picks up some kickin’ legs.
