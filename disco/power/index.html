Peer and Near-Peer Competition

The Commissioner's editorial ranking (week-01). It is a judgment, published as one.

#1 Love Sutton Brocks (EMCO) Peer Competition model #1

0-0 · 0.0 PF

The most complete construction in Disco: Puka Nacua, Saquon Barkley, Brock Bowers and Rashee Rice supply elite skill-position anchors while Brock Purdy/Jordan Love are viable weekly Superflex starters. There are better QB rooms, but nobody else combines this much high-end talent across RB, WR and TE.

#2 VHS Cowboys (Juan) Peer Competition model #3

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 1 spot lower

Josh Allen plus Dak Prescott is the best starting QB combination in the league, which is an enormous head start in Superflex. A.J. Brown, Terry McLaurin and Marvin Harrison Jr. keep WR strong; the only reason this is not No. 1 is the thinner RB room.

#3 Tua Girls One Kupp (HOP) Peer Competition model #10

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 7 spots lower

Patrick Mahomes and Caleb Williams give HOP a much stronger weekly Superflex foundation than the automated No. 10 model-board rank suggests, while Bijan Robinson and Drake London provide elite skill-position upside. TE remains the obvious weak point, but Harold Fannin does not have to be a star for the rest of this lineup to score.

#4 Goff'n with the Boys (Sealed) Near-Peer Competition model #8

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 4 spots lower

Justin Herbert and Jared Goff are a high-floor Superflex pair, and De'Von Achane, Justin Jefferson and Tee Higgins give the lineup genuine difference-makers. The roster falls off faster at RB/TE than the top three, but its starters are substantially better than an eighth-place automated ranking suggests.

#5 Corn-Fed Fatties (Babe) Near-Peer Competition model #7

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 2 spots lower

Bo Nix and C.J. Stroud are a solid Superflex pairing, then Jonathan Taylor, Ashton Jeanty and Jaxon Smith-Njigba provide a strong skill-position core. Sam LaPorta is carrying a thin TE room, but there is enough balance here that very few weekly lineup spots should be outright liabilities.

#6 The Dude Abides (The Dude) Near-Peer Competition model #2

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 4 spots higher

Christian McCaffrey, Amon-Ra St. Brown and Trey McBride give The Dude three elite anchors, with Trevor Lawrence and Daniel Jones forming a workable if unspectacular QB pair. WR depth behind Amon-Ra/Zay Flowers is the clearest reason this team sits outside the top five.

#7 Double TDs (Double) Near-Peer Competition model #9

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 2 spots lower

Jalen Hurts and Jayden Daniels might be the nastiest QB pair in the league and can erase a lot of roster sins by themselves. The problem is that the sins are real: the site currently has the RB room 12th and WR room 10th, so the quarterbacks have to carry considerably more weight here than they do for the teams above.

#8 Statistical Anomalies (McLovin) Competitive but Flawed model #6

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 2 spots higher

Lamar Jackson is exactly the kind of player you build a Superflex roster around, and the No. 1 RB room gives him real support. The current No. 12 TE room and middling WR construction leave too many points concentrated in Lamar and the backs, but the ceiling remains contender-level.

#9 Hand me a White Claw (PITCH) Competitive but Flawed model #4

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 5 spots higher

Drake Maye and Baker Mayfield are a good Superflex pairing, while CeeDee Lamb, Chris Olave and Emeka Egbuka give the receiving corps enough ceiling. Almost every room lands around league-average, which makes this roster hard to exploit but also means there is no obvious second dominant position.

#10 Boutte QBs (DIP) Competitive but Flawed model #5

0-0 · 0.0 PF · model has them 5 spots higher

Ja'Marr Chase and Nico Collins form the league's No. 1 WR room, but Kyler Murray/Cam Ward is a less secure Superflex pairing than the teams above and the RB room currently ranks 10th. This can absolutely work; it just needs the expensive WR advantage to overcome multiple merely adequate starting slots.

#11 Secret Asian Man (POP) Competitive but Flawed model #11

0-0 · 0.0 PF

Joe Burrow gives POP a legitimate QB1 and James Cook/Kyren Williams provide useful RB strength, but Bryce Young/Michael Penix leave the second QB slot uncertain and no other room ranks as an obvious league advantage. Kyle Pitts' Week 1 setup provides one potential path to outperform this ranking immediately.

#12 Stafford and Sons (Fingers) Strategic Reassessment Required model #12

0-0 · 0.0 PF

Jahmyr Gibbs, Derrick Henry and Bucky Irving may be the best collection of running backs in the league, which makes last place look ridiculous until you remember this is Superflex. The site's current evidence has Stafford 12th at QB and 12th at WR; Matthew Stafford/Sam Darnold cannot give away that much ground every week and expect the running backs to repair it.
