Week 7: BYEpocalypse Now

We’re almost already halfway through the fantasy season, and it’s been a banger so far, especially if your players aren’t all banged up. Very few of us have escaped the injury train this year, so count yourself lucky if you’re high on this list. I’m definitely not jinxing any of your players by listing you higher, or secretly feeding you karma if you’re lower. If you disagree with the rankings, let me know in league chat and we can have an unproductive but hopefully entertaining discussion about it.

1. George and Friends (DIP) Record: 11-1 Strengths: TE, RB, WR Weakness: QB Forecast: Playoff lock Team Overview: DIP took some gambles with this team. Some of them worked out (Daniel Jones, CMC, Tucker Kraft, and Pickens), and some of them did not (Kyler, every rookie RB except “Bill” JCM, Travis Hunter, and Ja’Marr Chase). Some, like Emeka Egbuka, have been stolen by injury. Regardless, DIP’s team has an embarrassment of riches at TT led by Trey McBride, who looked great with Kyler weighing him down this week. Despite Burrow’s injury, and because of CD’s extended absence, Chase and Pickens give him strong options at WR, even though Travis Hunter hasn’t quite lived up to the hype this season. If Kyler had worked out, then his QB1 wouldn’t be Daniel Jones, whose QB rating so far this year (104.4) is the highest it’s even been by a full 11.9 points (92.5 in 2022 with the NYJ). And just like the jets miss having a competent QB, DIP’s team is also scrambling, filling in with Bryce Young (who only does well against the Cowboys’ non-existent DST), and Spencer Rattler of the cursed Saints 2025 team. Woof. However, despite all the injuries, I like DIP’s chances for the playoffs, especially if Egbuka is able to get/stay healthy after his week 6 injury.

2. Hand Me a White Claw (Pitch) Record: 6-6 Strengths: WR, QB Weakness: RB Forecast: Playoff fringe Team Overview: If Daniel Jones is budget Josh Allen, then Baker Mayfield is budget Patrick freakin’ Mahomes. What a fun player to watch, and to have leading up your QB room. Despite the Bears’ best effort to almost lose every game, Caleb Williams has been having a good year. Unfortunately, the only starter Pitch has behind those two is Dillon Gabriel of the Browns, who may be one of the worst franchises in football. If the Browns play either the Titans or the Jets, that will be truly nauseating, nigh-unwatchable football. JSN has exploded this year, and leads a WR room that’s surprisingly deep, with Olave and Doubs staying healthy and providing weekly value. When CD gets back, her WR room will be undoubtedly the best in our league. Unfortunately, it’s counterbalanced by an RB room that is truly Halloween spooky. After Skattebo, who’s been admittedly awesome, it’s barren: Pacheco and Henderson haven’t worked out, Chubb is no longer producing his namesake, and Kareem Hunt and Michael Carter prove that quantity over quality just doesn’t work in FF roster construction. It’ll be tough sledding for this team until CD gets back, but after that (as long as Baker and Caleb stay healthy), her team is a contender, as long as she can overcome the early losses.

3. Jackson 5 (Fingers) Record: 5-7 Strengths: QB, RB Weaknesses: TE, WR Forecast: Playoff contender Team Overview: Just like Pitch, Fingers has suffered from early star injuries; Lamar Jackson is just as critical to this team as it is to the Ravens, so he’ll be looking to rebound off his early losses to contend for a playoff spot and the championship. In the meantime, he’s looking to Jordan Love and Matt Stafford to provide depth behind Jackson. Stafford’s week 8 BYE may be a problem if Lamar doesn’t start after this week’s BYE. At running back, Josh Jacobs continues to impress in Green Bay, and has put up solid numbers all season. Behind him, Rachaad White and Dobbins are solid contributors, and when Bucky Irving gets back from his injury, his RB room will be the most stable in the league. Unfortunately, the WR room is where it gets ugly. Jaylen Waddle hasn’t been ascendant since Tyreek’s injury, and DJ Moore, Matthew Golden, and Luther Burden III have all underperformed expectations. That’s what you get for over-investing in Bears receiving options. If Mike Evans hadn’t gone out early with a hamstring, he’d be feeling a lot better about his WR room. Speaking about over-investing in Bears receiving options, Colston Loveland still has a spot on Fingers’ bench, backing up Zach Ertz in one of the league’s worst-smelling TE rooms. Gross. Despite these weaknesses, a return from Mike Evans to Tampa Bay’s electric offense may propel him (and the Bucs) into the playoffs, and perhaps a championship.

4. The Dude Abides Record: 7-5 Strength: WR Weaknesses: TE, QB Forecast: Playoffs likely Team Overview: Let’s start with the good news: Justin Jefferson Drake London, and Puka Nacua are an all-star assemblage at WR; well, were, since Puka’s going to miss some time. And while Courtland Sutton hasn’t quite lived up to all expectations in Denver, having him on your team as your WR3/4 is not bad at all. I thought Pitch’s QB options were poor until I looked at The Dude’s. Mac Jones and Joe Flacco. Tyrod Taylor. Anthony (who broke his eye with a resistance band) Richardson. Need I say more? I guess after the fart-fest that was Jake Browning in Cincy, Flacco stays the starter, though with his (lack of) pocket mobility and a porous offensive line, his days may be numbered. This QB room is Justin Fields’ level of incompetence. Running back options are middling but fine, with Kyren and ETN. If Chuba can take back the starting job from Rico after his injury, maybe prospects improve. Retired rapper Darren Waller rescued Pat Friermuth from having to start every week in The Dude’s TE slot, which is fortunate since Pittsburgh doesn’t appear to remember they can throw him the football. Over Pat’s last 4 NFL seasons, he’s never had more than 65 catches, 732 yards, and has scored only 2 or 7 TDs each year – numbers he’s not likely to reach or surpass this year. The Dude’s made a strong start, and his winning record and WR room could carry him forward into a decent playoff run.

5. Corn-Fed Fatties (Mr. Pitch) Record: 4-8 Strength: TE Weaknesses: Meh at other positions Forecast: Playoff fringe Team Overview: Tyler Warren is the star of this team and it’s not even close. The rookie is this year’s Brock Bowers or Sam LaPorta, and continues to perform in Indy. It’s mystifying that Babe would roster Hockenson, Barner, Gesicki, and Likely behind him, because when would you start them? Likely may crack into the lineup on Warren’s week 11 BYE I guess. Week 8 is gonna be rough with both Charbonnet and Jeanty on BYEs, but the Cowboys are only a sprained ankle away from having to start Jaydon Blue, so maybe he and Quinshon can get it done that week. Maybe roster a few dart throws at RB instead of useless TEs? For WR, ARSB and Tet are the headliners, followed up by Ayomanor and a distant MHJ. Oh what might have been in Arizona… And if Godwin returns to form in Tampa, his WR room will improve substantially. Until then, not a lot of depth or options here. Dak leads a QB room that is middle-of-the pack, with Dart and Rodgers providing help at both ends of the age spectrum. JJ McCarthy is the emergency QB here. The Fatties have gotten unlucky with some of their earlier matchups, but depending on how the next few weeks shake out, they may waddle their way into the playoffs.

6. Nabers with Benefits (POP) Record: 8-4 Strength: QB Weaknesses: RB, WR Forecast: Playoff lock Team Overview: He’s got one of the better records, but the reason he’s so low in the power rankings is because injuries have not been kind. His team’s namesake (along with Tyreek Hill), have fallen, which is why his WR room is in shambles. He’s the TB of our league, but somehow keeps getting it done; his Baker Mayfields are Jayden Daniels and Drake Maye, who have continued to produce for fantasy with rushing and passing prowess. The waiver wire will have to fill in some talent on weeks 12 and 14 BYE weeks, since Pops is only rostering 2. If Breece hall were on any other team than the Jets (except maybe the Titans), he’d be better than the RB2 he’s currently performing as. Behind him, D’Andre Swift and Woody Marks aren’t getting it done, and RJ Harvey (same age as Breece) has not delivered on the offseason hype or draft capital. In the WR room, Deebo has been getting it done in Washington, while Ladd McConkey’s been overshadowed by Quege, who was out last week with everyone’s favorite WR muscle, the hamstring. Diggs and Boutte have had their moments, but otherwise the rest of the WR room (10 whole roster spots; 50% of all his roster) is unremarkable. Pops had a strong start to the season, but without Nabers and Tyreek, any further injury could derail his playoff hopes and championship chances.

7. All Barkley No Bite (Sealed) Record: 7-5 Strength: WR Weakness: QB Forecast: Playoffs likely Team Overview: Sealed’s team is another strong starter with somewhat brighter playoff prospects, as he hasn’t sustained any major injuries. Odunze is the dude in Chicago, and Texan Nico Collins has been getting it done despite mediocre QB play. Worthy has been decent, though we’ll see how that changes with Rashee Rice coming off his suspension in KC. Unluckily, this team doesn’t have a Mahomes. Instead, it’s Jared Goff leading CJ Stroud and a surprisingly-decent Sam Darnold into battle weekly. Week 8’s gonna be rough for this team, as Goff and Darnold share a BYE. If Stroud can repeat his performance against the Cowboys (unlikely), then he’ll start to feel a bit better about his QB options, but at three-deep middling talent, it’s tough. Saquon has underperformed expectations, and has fewer yards on the year through six games (325) than Rico Dowdle had in the last two (389). KWIII has been ceding snaps to Charbonnet, and the rest of the RB room is not notable, except for the fact that he’s rostering another Kenneth (Gainwell). Sammy LaP hasn’t been as great as his rookie season, but leads a shallow TE room (Dalton Kinkaid) into battle, until he goes on BYE week 8. Once again, Sealed’s team has started strong, but with Chuba coming back, Saquon’s underperformance, and a likely week 8 loss due to BYEs (Goff, Darnold, KWIII, LaPorta), there’s a chance his playoff dreams could derail.

8th Place (HOP) Record: 8-4 Strength: RB Weaknesses: TE, QB Forecast: Playoffs likely Team Overview: You know what they say about expectations and performance. If you name your team 8th Place, that’s probably where you’re gonna end up. Hop may have one of the best starting records, but it may be tough sledding the rest of the way with QBs like Cam Ward and Carson Wentz pulling behind Justin Herbert, who’s underperformed expectations this year. Justin Herbert’s got the dawg in him, but he ain’t no Balto. Bijan, OTOH is that dog for this team. He and Achane are the feature backs here, though talent drops off quick here (Jordan Mason, Tuten, and Spears). Still, rostering these dart throws could pay off if any of their teams’ primary RBs goes down. At WR it’s good-not-great, with Rashee Rice finally coming back to the Chiefs. AJB has been a disappointment this year, as Jalen Hurts has seemingly forgotten how to throw the football, and with Philly’s recent offensive struggles in general. The guy just loves his tush pushes, and AJB’s been a third wheel on this offense. Kelce and Goedert are low-end TE1s week in and out, which is to say they’re generally not moving the needle in his matchups. As long as the top dogs on this team stay healthy, there’s a good chance to make the playoffs with his strong start, but unless his QBs and TEs have a freak performance, getting to and winning the championship will be an uphill battle.

9. VHS Cowboys (Juan) Record: 3-9 Strength: QB Weaknesses: TE, RB, WR, Depth Forecast: Toilet bowl contender Team Overview: Juan’s team is going the way of the Cowboys this year, which is to say, poorly. Josh Allen is the crown jewel of this team, and everyone else is a Swarovski crystal. He’s the Alpha, and everyone else is an omega. He’s the Breitling, and everyone else is a Casio. Hail to the king, and hell to everyone else on this roster. I guess it’s not totally true – James Cook has been good in Buffalo too, but Tony P and Ollie G have been helping only marginally more than Aaron Jones Sr. and Trey B. James Cook is the needle in a dusty haystack here, and you need more than a needle if you’re going to be poking bears for a playoff spot. It’s a bear market in the WR room as well. BTJ has not performed up to the expectations he set at the end of last year, and DeVonta Smith has been having the same issues as AJB, but worse. Behind them things get really ugly. Like Calvin Ridley and Wan’Dale Robinson ugly. Once again, we have a quantity over quality approach here: 11 WRs (55% of the roster!), but no WR1 ROS, and every single other dude is WR42 or worse. And still, he chooses to keep Jalen Nailor and Ryan Flournoy on the bench. Maybe they’re good locker room guys? The worst TE room in the league (Harold Fannin and Dalton Schultz) round out a team that’s reliant on two players for the majority of its points. Unless the environments in Jacksonville and Philly change, this recent champ has a snowball’s chance of winning out.

10. Kamara Sutra (EMCO) Record: 3-9 Strengths: Persistence and personality Weaknesses: WR, TE, Depth Forecast: Toilet bowl contender Team Overview: The position of this Kamara Sutra is bringing pain, not pleasure. Just like Juan, she’s got two feathers in an otherwise-dilapidated, sagging cap: Patrick Mahomes and Jahmyr Gibbs – great players, but poor supporting cast. Let’s unpack this sad sack. If Brock Purdy hadn’t gone down with a toe injury back in, this RB room would look much brighter, but right now Tua and Kirk are hanging on by a thread – one a concussion away from not being available, and the other a concussion away from being startable, respectively. Bad vibes either way. Terry McLaurin and Garrett Wilson may be good WR1s in Washington and New York (respectively), but if they’re your fantasy team’s WR1/2, you’re in trouble. It quickly gets worse: Darius Slayton, Kyle Williams, Jalen Tolbert, and two Ravens (Bateman and Hopkins) aren’t going to get it done. EMCO’s praying for a nuclear return for Lamar Jackson to spark life into this Frankenstein of a roster. How many top-12 finishes has Alvin Kamara had this year? Top 24? How many TDs has he scored? I’ll set the over-under at 1.5. Did you take the over? You lose, just like EMCO has in 9 of 12 matchups. He has zero top-12 finishes; 17 and 19 are the closest he’s come, and those are his only top-24 finishes. The rest: 26, 33, 37, 32. That’s just NYJ-level performance right there, and you don’t like it for your RB2 behind Gibbs. Najee Harris still remains on this roster for sentimental reasons, I guess – maybe that AETC SNCO life is just too busy to take the rudder to save a sinking fantasy team. Cade Otton and Mark Andrews round out this roster. One hopes Mark Andrews returns to form, but it doesn’t seem Likely, and the most exciting thing about Otton is that he plays for Tampa grabbing Baker’s balls. We drafted early so that EMCO could join us for the draft, but maybe going on auto would have left her with a better team this year. Or maybe she just has Jets fever and wants to manage her fantasy team like the franchise manages their real-life squad.

11. Double TDs (Double) Record: 3-9 Strength: Great WD instructor Weaknesses: TE, Depth Forecast: Toilet bowl contender Team Overview: The only way this team makes the playoffs is if they start scoring double the TDs. Oh what might have been this season for Double and his TDs. If only Joe Burrow hadn’t gone out week two with a toe injury. If only Justin Fields had lived up to the hype. If only Davante Adams and Derrick Henry were 3-4 years younger. If only Chase Brown had repeated last year’s performance. If only Ricky Pearsall had continued his end-of-last-season breakout and not gotten injured. This team is full of broken promises and unfulfilled dreams; their theme song is “I Dreamed a Dream” from Les Miserables. Like Fantine, this team is unlikely to enjoy a happily-ever-after. Fields, Penix, Burrow, and recently-benched Cooper Rush. Enough said. Chase Brown, David Montgomery, Rhamondre Stevenson, and both Justice Hill and Derrick Henry. The King can only put so many people on his back, and the best thing about the rest is that David Montgomery plays for the Lions, a known man/dawg team. 48, 9, 20, 19, 27, 47 – Those are Davante Adams’ finishes this week. If your WR1 isn’t outscoring your TE1, you’ve either drafted a top-3 TE, or you’ve made questionable WR1 choices. Sure, Jake Ferguson has been unexpectedly excellent in the absence of CD in Dallas, 44 for 305and 4 TDs), but Davante’s only pulled in 26 for 396 and 3. Flexing Ferg or Kittle is luxury at TE, but poverty at WR and RB. 3-9 is a tough start, and it’s not going to get easier, unless Justin Fields suddenly Jalen Hurtses himself mid-season and learns to throw accurate footballs instead of existing a slightly more accurate, less strong, somehow-startable, two-eyed version of Anthony Richardson.

Conclusion Why only 11 teams? Because we all know my team’s the best and is gonna dominate the rest of the way. Or, to make sure I’m not reverse-karma-ing myself, I’ll stack on some injuries and be irrelevant. Who knows? It’s fantasy football and it’s unpredictable. Any given Sunday could turn our teams’ chances around, but it’s still fun (and maddening) to play. Thanks for sticking around another year, and best of luck to everyone except my opponent any given week.
