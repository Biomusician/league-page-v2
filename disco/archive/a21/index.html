2021 Disco Week 1 Preview/Draft Review

Disco Chat Vol 3.I “What Do You Mean, Superflex?!”

Hopes and Dreams Your Team Can Be Amazing A fantasy team before the season is like a newborn child. They have limitless potential; could be a star soccer player, programmer, or president. They could solve world hunger, bring world peace, and bring home the fantasy championship. True, they’ll have to fight through injuries, the waiver wire, BYE weeks, and whatever the real-life analogy to those things is (look, it’s not a perfect metaphor), but all on the way to greatness. Alternatively, some newborns don’t have anything going for them. Some of you drafted crack babies, and it’s going to take a lot of work to get them on the right path. In any case, love the one you’re with. Everyone thinks the baby they made is amazing, so y’all enjoy raising them this season. Just know that when you say “This is little QB Nursing Home, isn’t he amazing?!” that I’m coming back with my standard baby-praise fishing response “Wow, that’s a baby!”

The Draft in Review Definitely a Good/Bad/Ugly Situation First, can we take a moment to appreciate Superflex (SF)? Or maybe a few seasons? Especially in a 10-team league, the SF format values the QB position, and allows for more interesting decisions - that was also the rationale behind increasing the bench spots and allowing for 4 starting FLEX positions instead of just 2. So let’s see how y’all chose to posture your teams to fill these holes… #sts In no particular order: QB Nursing Home (Juanton) The fact that we now have four Cowboys fans in the league is a point of great personal pride for me, but it does mean I have to compete with two other people for my homer picks, Juan being one of them. Fortunately, he didn’t draft a single Boy in Blue this year in this league favoring elite WR talent and ancient QBs. Aaron Rodgers and Ben Roethlisberger are basically the muppet critics at this point in their careers - hopefully they’ll last you the entire season! Team Heroes: Davante Adams and DeAndre Hopkins Weakest Links: TE, FLEX, RB, and Bench Draft Decision Points: Sticking with WR in the 3rd with Hopkins vs drafting a guy like Najee Harris or Austin Ekeler, then drafting Miles Sanders over AJB, Calvin Ridley, and Justin Jefferson. Drafting James Robinson over Allen Robinson was the wrong Robinson call; one of them is a WR1, and one of them is coached by Urban Meyer. Bold Prediction: You overcome the positional skill gaps via smart waiver wire adds, and make it all the way to the ‘Ship, where you lose. Why You Win Week 1: Hopkins and Adams combine for over 70 points, and McLovin just can’t put together enough to overcome the gap between them and Calvin Ridley/Tyler Lockett. You called your shot on Goedert, and he rewards you with over 80 yards and a TD, as Jalen Hurts take a step forward in his passing and Goedert finds his place in the offense. Browns for the Win (PITCH Hand) Returning with a vengeance from her last place finish last year, PITCH starts strong by asking what SF is during the draft. While she can take some solace in sabotaging Babe’s draft with an early Brown selection, her own draft leaves some opportunity for improvement. I like how she started RB/QB/TE, securing one of the top-3 TE talents in a balanced start, before continuing that trend of not selecting a player from the same position in consecutive rounds until rounds 8 and 9, where she picked up MT/JuJu. Team Heroes: Zeke, Kyler, and Waller Weakest Links: RB, FLEX, and Bench Draft Decision Points: Picking MT and JuJu back to back is a decision I hate. Both of them are high-risk options, and choosing MT over Tee Higgins, Jerry Jeudy, Chase Claypool, and Brandon Aiyuk just hurts to type. If JuJu scores more fantasy points than Jaylen Waddle this year, I will be in shock. Good snag of Justin Fields in the 7th! Picking Waddle and Mooney was great too, but I’m no fan of Randall Cobb or Desean Jackson. Bold Prediction: Will be in the running for the first back-to-back Disco Downbeat award winner, repeating her 2020 last-place performance, but will make enough waiver moves to outmaneuver Juan and HOP. Why You Win Week 1: Despite being unfavored against Trophy Wife, Kyler Murray, AJB, Robert Woods, and your positional advantage at TE carry you to victory. Two TDs from Waller put you over the edge, and Julio Jones doesn’t significantly affect AJB’s target share in Tennessee. You win despite, not because of, JuJu. 8th Place (HOP Christensen) He called his shot with his team name, but I think he’ll be fighting to finish higher. His team is relatively balanced, but there are still some weak spots at RB and TE. Team Heroes: Saquads and Lamar Jackson Weakest Links: TE, Starters, and Bench Draft Decision Points: HOP chose to wait for his 2nd RB until round 4, and was rewarded with CEH, who is projected to finish around RB20 this year - so the gamble paid off! His ceiling is higher in an electric KC offense, and I’m sure HOP is happy drafting a Chief onto his roster. Hurts in the 6th has huge upside to turn the corner this year in Philly, especially with new WR weapons. Maybe he can do a Josh Allen and fix his accuracy issues in 1 year! I wouldn’t have picked Golladay over Diontae Johnson, but it’s not egregious, and his pickup of Mike Davis in the 8th is great value for the Falcons RB1. Loading up on Viska, Ruggs, and Pittman are also upside picks that show he did his research. Kudos! Bold Prediction: Will finish better than 8th, and will not repeat his last-place finish from 2019. Why You Win Week 1: Saquon comes off his injury like a new man, and bowls over the Broncos defenders on his way to a 2TD game. CEH shows out in KC, finally cementing himself in the offense alongside Kelce and Tyreek as an elite option. Kenny Golladay also manages to overcome preseason injury, providing an elite receiving option to Daniel Jones, who is accurate enough to propel Kenny G to WR2 status. Trophy Wife (EMCO McDonough) The other half of the other fantasy-playing couple in our league drafted a surprisingly solid team, considering that she didn’t understand SF before things started. Fortunately, she benefited from my and Glory’s thoughtful recommendations throughout the in-person draft… just kidding! We just made fun of her draft picks incessantly and didn’t offer any help. If history is any indication, she’ll be trolling the waiver wire early and often to get some value and depth on her team early, and she fights to earn a trophy. Team Heroes: Jonathan Taylor and Nick Chubb Weakest Links: QB, TE, Starters, and WR Draft Decision Points: Going RB-RB in a SF league is aggressive. Going RB-RB-QB-RB is bonkers. Allen Robinson is a solid picking at the tail end of the 5th, but ol’ “Drops” Chase as her WR2 gives me pause. Still, I love Diontae Johnson as a WR2 in the 7th, and Deebo could ball out for her if he can stay healthy. Stafford/Cousins as your QBs doesn’t fill me with confidence, but Fitzpatrick could present some upside in the WFT throwing to McLaurin. Bold Prediction: Will make playoffs as a middling team, and lose in the quarterfinals. Why You Win Week 1: JT and Nick Chubb provide RB1 numbers for you, while PITCH’s Zeke still looks slow and old from last season. Waller fades into the background behind Ruggs and Edwards breakout, and Josh Jacobs runs wild. Meanwhile, Deebo becomes the talent we always knew he was with a transcendent week 1 performance, and Ja’Marr Chase reacclimates to football in an explosive week 1 performance against the Vikings, scoring his first NFL TD. Corn-Fed Fatties (Babe Hand) Despite the fast-PITCH draft sabotage, Babe ended up with the better team. He favored stiff-arming QB, which works well in 1QB leagues, while taking RBs early and often. Time will tell whether this approach works out or not! Team Heroes: Alvin Kamara and Justin Herbert Weakest Links: QB, FLEX, and WR Draft Decision Points: I love Herbert, but taking him over Dak and Russ was criminal, especially with Tyreek on the board. Love Justin Jefferson in the late 4th, Devonta Smith in the 8th, and Courtland Sutton in the 9th, even though there are questions about how many targets he’ll demand coming back from his injury. Love Antonio Brown at 12.7, but picking Irv Smith at 14.7 when he’s out for the season was the most Taco moment of the draft. This was likely a beverage-assisted pick, and it wasn’t even as egregious as Baker Mayfield in the 5th. Bold Prediction: If your QBs were Dak and Tannehill instead of Herbert and Baker, you would have a much better season. Why You Win Week 1: Austin Ekeler catches 7 passes for a couple TDs, while Justin Jefferson continues to shine in Minnesota. Meanwhile, Courtland Sutton reminds everyone why he was a top prospect, coming off his injury with a vengeance to give a WR1 performance opposite Jerry Jeudy. DeVonta Smith gives us a sneak peek of his talent with a 90 yard game, though one of his TDs gets called back due to holding. The Dude Abides (Glory McDonough/The Dude) The Dude starts out his LtCol run by delegating his team draft to one of his most trusted NCOs. The tasker didn’t flow as is tradition from the O-5 through the hallway, then the CGOs, then the shop chief, then the NCO, then his team of Amn, but at least it fell to a highly-capable individual. We’ll see if he can build off the positive momentum heading into the regular season, or whether his team will go the way of Pompeii, buried under a volcano of injuries, underperformers, and poor adds. Team Heroes: CMC, Dak, and Kittle Weakest Links: WR, FLEX, and Bench Draft Decision Points: Taking Kittle means you may end up weak at WR or RB, but taking Montgomery in the 4th and CeeDee in the 5th/Kupp in the 7th means that The Dude has some solid options. While I disagree with taking CD over Keenan and Allen Robinson, I can understand the hype! Later round adds I like include Aiyuk, Elijah Moore, and pre-season sensation Rhamondre Stevenson. Bold Prediction: While set up with a solid team, The Dude gets caught up in his PCS, house-hunting, and new Tinker duties, and slides to the Toilet Bowl, where he battles to not slide from Hero to Zero in consecutive years. Why You Win Week 1: Dak and CMC were just too much for the Corn-Fed Fatties, combining for 7 TDs and 500 yards. CeeDee Lamb continues his breakout campaign, and while Babe is happy to see his Cowboys dominate, it results in his destruction, since CD takes 2 to the house. Sniffin’ Wins (Fingers Cooke) After back-to-back strong performances, Fingers is looking for his first championship in the league. He had a good draft, and since he’s no longer in the military, maybe he has a lot of time on his hands to actively manage his team to victory. Or, maybe he’s even busier, and that desk downtime he used to have will mean his team gets demoted in favor of real-life duties. In any case, this will be a defining season for the Sniffers. Team Heroes: Derrick “King” Henry and Tracis Kelce Weakest Links: WR, QB, and FLEX Draft Decision Points: Clearly we have a Pats homer on our hands, because picking grandpa Brady over Stafford, Tannehill, and Hurts is crazy otherwise. I guess I can see denying Juan the chance to draft another old dude. I hate Mike Evans this year, even though he’s given us seven 1,000 yard seasons - the TB WR corps is too stacked this year; taking him over Justin Jefferson, Scary Terry, Keenan, and Allen Robinson is definitely choice. Amari Cooper in the 6th and Jacobs in the 5th are good picks, and I like Jeudy in the 8th, breakout candidate Higbee in the 10th, and Tyler Boyd as a value Bengals WR pick in the 11th. LASIK Jameis in the 15th could be a league-winning play, if he can find the yards and TDs he had two years ago, while avoiding the interceptions. Bold Prediction: Like bloodhounds, the Sniffers find their way to another fantasy championship, where they finally overcome their opponent to carve their name into the Disco history books. Why You Win Week 1: Josh Jacobs silences his naysayers with a dominant week 1 performance against the Ravens D. Derrick Henry shows no signs of slowing down in his age-27 season, and Tom Brady continues to mock age by balling out with his old friend Gronk. Sealed has Pitts, but the rookie TE fails to impress in his debut, especially opposite Kelce’s 25 fantasy points. How ‘Bout Them Vaqueros! How ‘bout Sealed rolling in at the 11th hour to save the league in the face of Double’s last-minute exit?! We appreciate the sub, but we kinda wish we had Double back, since he wouldn’t have drafted nearly as good of a team. Where everyone else was flailing for SF picks, Sealed just sat back sniping the values, prevening them from reaching the other savvy drafters. Can the new kid on the block win it all in his first season in Disco? Team Heroes: Dalvin Cook, Tyreek Hill, Russ Weakest Links: QB and Bench Draft Decision Points: Waiting for QB until round 3 is risky in SF, and while Russ in the 3rd is highway robbery, he’s betting big on Trevor Lawrence (6.8) being able to produce with an awful offensive line, and a (potential) clown for a coach (thanks Urban Meyer). This ain’t college, baby! History has shown us that TEs need a couple years to develop in the league, but the Vaqueros were comfortable taking rookie hyped Kyle Pitts above talent like McLaurin, David Montgomery, Keenan Allen, and Allen Robinson. DJ Moore and Chase Claypool are exiting in the 7th/8th, and Javonte Williams presents some latter-half upside behind MGIII in Denver. Mike Willaims (10.8) will be great if healthy, and there is a lot of hype surrounding Zach Wilson (12.8) in New York. Jalen Reagor (17.3) needs to show he has it this year, or he’s likely an early cut candidate. Bold Prediction: Sealed makes a strong run, but a surprisingly poor showing in the quarterfinals means he’ll have to try again next season for the ‘Ship. Why You Win Week 1: The Seahawks let Russ cook, and he throws 4 passing TDs. Dalvin Cook shows everyone why he is a candidate for the #1RB by steamrolling a Cinci offense that just can’t get going: 150 yards and 2 TDs. Tyreek and Keenan, DJ Moore and Chase Claypool are too much for the mixed bag on Sniffin Win’s bench, and the Vaqueros edge out Fingers in the week 1 opener. Just Can’t Bear It His name is likely a reference to Andy Dalton starting week 1 against the Rams, where they get crushed into oblivion; or, maybe it was because he missed the brisket, ribs, and pulled pork on Saturday. Either way, it’s tough to bear the first overall pick, but the season will bear out whether his selections were solid, or whether he’ll need to bear down on the waiver wire as his starters are laid bare to injury and BYEs. Team Heroes: Patty Mahomes and Tony Gibson Weakest Link: RB Draft Decision Points: Waiting so long between picks is a curse of the turn, but I bet DIP was happy to see Gibson (2.10) make it back to him, along with elite option Stefon Diggs (3.1). Pairing each of them Carson (5.1) and McLaurin (4.10) must feel good too, though I like Robinson over McLaurin this year. Later (6.10), he took a chance on Julio opposite AJB in TEN, and then splurged for a breakout TE candidate in TJ Hockenson (7.1). Gus Edwards and Damien Harris are both starting RBs, so getting them at the 8/9 turn is value, and he bolstered QB with Tua and Darnold, who are both looking to turn the corner after poor showings last year. Love Robby Anderson at 11.1, Mac Jones at 15.1, Jakobi Meyers at 16.10, Callaway at 17.1, and my guy Blake Jarwin at 19.1. Bold Prediction: A perpetual talent in the league, he makes it to the quarterfinals again, but has an unlucky showing once one of his starters gets injured the week before the game. Why You Win Week 1: Patrick Mahomes is the 1.1 in SF for a reason, and even though 8th Place has Lamar Jackson starting opposite, Dip’s strong WR corps of Stefon Diggs, McLauren, and Julio Jones are too much. Hockenson, though he’s on a terrible Lions team, is the #1 target on the team, and pushes Dip’s team to a narrow victory over HOP and the Placers. Kmet Me Bro Can we just for a moment admit that it’s ridiculous how close this guy is to the commissioner? Not only was he completely prepared for the wonky roster construction and “superflex” format, but he hosted the draft party at the commish’s house. I’m calling clear favoritism here. The fact that he won in 2019 is bull, and I believe it’s under FTC investigation. I’d be careful associating with this guy. Team Heroes: Cole Kmet, Trey Lance, and Corey Davis Weakest Link: TE Draft Decision Points: Bold Prediction: Will win the championship handily, with the best regular-season record. Why You Win Week 1: Josh Allen, Aaron Jones, and Najee Harris are a power trio that Juan just can’t stop/won’t stop. Even though he has the WR positional advantage, I’ve got the depth, and my bench will show up to push me over the top.
