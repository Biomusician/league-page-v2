Common Tactical Picture · Week 1

Corn-Fed Fatties (Babe) vs Double TDs (Double)

FEATURE

Babe’s team is a 97th percentile baby; you know the one - nine months old, built like a bag of concrete, sleeps eleven hours a night and eats whatever hits the plastic portions plate. Three positional rooms, and only one of them is soft: 9th at TE, but 4/6th at WR/RB. On the other hand, Double TDs is a newborn. A newborn’s head runs about a quarter of its body length and the legs are strictly there for anatomical correctness, which is Double’s roster to a T - QB room 3rd, tight ends 4th, and a backfield ranked 12th of 12. Woof. Gorgeous baby dome but ornamental legs. The thing about newborns, though, is that they eventually learn to walk (and maybe run), and they generally get it done well before Week 14. Double just needs it to happen before kickoff.

Statistical Anomalies (McLovin) vs Secret Asian Man (POP)

MAJOR

POP built a perfectly average baby. All moving parts and internal organs are in place, but the normal reaction from most people will just be a polite “What a baby!” He’s 5th at RB, 7th at TE, and 11th at QB, thanks to Burrow, Bryce, and Penix. He dropped Tyreek Hill (his last pick) to claim AJ Barner, which is like putting underwear on an infant - mostly a waste of time. Ask him in December whether Bryce and Penix somehow found a way to make themselves relevant, and whether Burrow can remain healthy. We may not have kickers in this league, but I still found a way to punt with the TE position. Just like most babies, I’ll be streaming out of at least one orifice (the waiver wire) for the foreseeable future. However, my RBs and QBs (4/4 of my first picks) are great! My baby has the constitution of a teenager, but the WR/TE focus of a two year-old. My fix? Claiming Mike Gesicki off waivers, which is about as effective as yelling “Improve! Learn!” at a small child.

Tua Girls One Kupp (HOP) vs VHS Cowboys (Juan)

MAJOR

HOP and Juan brought similar babies to the party, just in different onesies. You know the types of parents - the ones who tell you their kid rolled over at three months, and then keep repeating it to everyone because it’s literally the only thing interesting about their baby. HOP’s trick is the WR room (2nd in the league, and genuinely wonderful with Drake London and Tet McMillan). Everything else is still gestating: 8th at RB, 9th at QB, 11th at TE. Juan’s “his first word was papa” is the QB room, and it is the best one in Disco, full stop (Josh Allen and his homey Dak). However, put that team down on the carpet and it just kinda flops around; you get 8th at TE and 11th at RB. His fix was five FAAB on Malik Davis, paid for by dropping Jerry Jeudy - steadying the high chair with a bit of bubblegum and duct tape. Both parents are going to spend Sunday saying “watch this, watch this!” and then just holding their one-tricks up. Whoever’s works best wins.

The Dude Abides (The Dude) vs Goff'n with the Boys (Sealed)

STANDARD

The Dude registered for everything and received three of each. Three tight ends, especially when one of them is Trey McBride, is like a crib for three different rooms - excessive. Dalton Kincaid and Pat Freiermuth are in the kitchen and basement. He still has 1st-ranked TE room I guess, and six running backs as well, headlined by Christian McCaffrey (Questionable, naturally) and Cam Skattebo. All of it was financed by the WR room, which is Amon-Ra St. Brown and then a closet of hand-me-downs, 11th in Disco. However, Sealed went the other direction entirely and assembled a receiving corps eligible for a group discount: Justin Jefferson, Tee Higgins, Chris Godwin and Keenan Allen in his thirteenth season. His tight end room is Hunter Henry, Dallas Goedert and Cade Otton, which are all onesies from the local thrift store. Sealed’s “he’s very advanced for his age” is De’Von Achane, young talent on an otherwise mature team. Sleeper says 54/46, 140.36 to 133.97, second-closest game on the board.

Love Sutton Brocks (EMCO) vs Hand me a White Claw (PITCH)

STANDARD

EMCO is the mom who gave two of her kids the same name: Love, Sutton, and two Brocks: Bowers and Purdy. The nursery is stocked to match: six running backs, led by Saquon Barkley and Josh Jacobs, good for the 2nd-ranked RB room, and Bowers anchoring the TE room tucks her in at 2nd there. The QB room lands 10th, which is what happens when your QB2 is Purdy (but not pretty) good. PITCH’s team is the third-born child: Nobody photographs anything anymore, the outlet covers are off, and sharp corners have become learning opportunities vs. pool noodle magnets. Her squad is a 19-pack of Sams Cola: 5th at WR, 5th at TE, 6th at QB, 7th at RB. Everything is fine and everything is middle-of-the-road. She’ll tell you the kid is happy and healthy and then change the subject to a more interesting topic like birdwatching or gardening. Sleeper says 57/43, 134.61 to 125.18.

Stafford and Sons (Fingers) vs Boutte QBs (DIP)

STANDARD

Both of these parents named the baby after its ugliest feature. Stafford and Sons is Matthew Stafford and Sam Darnold, a QB room that grades 12th of 12, which is what happens when you wait until the 7th round to draft a QB in SF. Fingers went ahead and adopted them anyway. He childproofed the nursery and the kitchen - (3 RBs and 3 WRs ion the first six rooms) but left the pool wide open (TE Kelce and Andrews). Instead of addressing positional need, he swapped Calvin Ridley for Zavion Thomas - two WRs. However, DIP went and had beaucoup (bonjour) QBs, four of them (Kyler, Cam Ward, Briskett and D-Watson) for two starting slots, one of whom who won’t start without some serious roster massaging. What actually keeps that baby alive are Ja’Marr Chase and Nico Collins, the 1st-ranked WR room in Disco, the first- and third-born children of his draft. And somehow he named his team after a 16th-round pick.
