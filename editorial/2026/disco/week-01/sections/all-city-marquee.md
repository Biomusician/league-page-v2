Raise the floor to 100,000 and this stops being a novelty and starts being a BRAC round. Same population of names, one new threshold, and everything under the line closes. Chase, Kansas closes. Robinson, Illinois closes. Aubrey, Texas, which cleared the last list's smallest tier by six residents, closes so hard that the best kicker in football is now unemployable.

That last one is the real bill. The qualifying kicker pool in the entire National Football League is two men and they're both named Tyler. Tyler Loop kicks for Baltimore at K6, Tyler Bass kicks for Buffalo at K20, and there is nobody else. If Loop pulls something in warmups we're starting the other Tyler. If they both go down we're going for it on fourth down until January.

Everywhere else the bill lands somewhere between one round and four. Ja'Marr Chase, third overall, becomes Drake London, fourteenth. Bijan Robinson, second overall, becomes Omarion Hampton, nineteenth. Jonathan Taylor, eighth, becomes Bucky Irving, fifty-fourth. Colston Loveland, fortieth, becomes Tyler Warren, fifty-second, and Warren's groin is why I keep refreshing the wire. This team still wins games. It just wins them off a worse board.

London means the one in England, because London, Ohio is 10,279 people and the four-country clause finally had something to do after sitting in the rulebook doing nothing. And Washington is here on a written exception: the Census Bureau's own District of Columbia guide says the District has one city, its population glossary says the District is a county, and I picked the reading that got me a receiver.
