## Draft Power Rankings: Ten Force Designs, Assessed

Every draft is a force design, and this is the design review. Method first:
all 150 picks measured against the FantasyPros half-PPR consensus board
(snapshot 2026-08-29); a team's score is the sum of its deltas, positive
when value fell to you, negative when you bought early. The counterargument
is real and conceded up front: consensus measures the market, and wars are
won by the force you field, so a minus number attached to the right roster
is just the cost of conviction. Ten designs, assessed in order.

### 1. Roster 4 (+1)

The only balanced budget in the league, at plus-1. Marvin Harrison 20 picks
late, zero defenses in fifteen picks, and the lone kicker mistake (Eddy
Pineiro, minus-76) nearly erased an otherwise spotless sheet.
Divest-and-hope, executed with discipline.

### 2. Roster 10 (-42)

Trevor Lawrence at a 37-pick discount and Josh Jacobs late. The design
holds one assumption per capability area instead of five, which is why the
bill stayed small. The Fairbairn kicker premium (minus-80) is the smudge.

### 3. SHACtin a fool (-115)

Bijan Robinson plus an Atlanta triple is a concentrated bet on one offense,
and Jaxson Dart 23 picks late hedges the quarterback risk for cheap. Two
defenses is one more than doctrine requires, and Seattle at minus-68 was
the expensive one.

### 4. Roster 2 (-137)

Two different city triples (Chicago and Denver), which is a wargame where
two scenarios decide everything. Caleb Williams 29 picks late funds part of
it. High variance, priced accordingly.

### 5. Wild SeeKats (-143)

The most conventional force structure in the league: one of everything,
nothing exotic, no assumption stacked on another. Minus-143 almost entirely
from paying early at kicker (Cam Little, minus-73). The design is sound;
the parts procurement needs work.

### 6. Swifty Mahomey (-167)

Landed the actual Patrick Mahomes at pick 114 after wearing his name all
August, then built a four-piece Rams block around Stafford anyway, which is
hedging your identity crisis at market rates. Two stacks show real theory
of victory. The Rams defense at minus-84 paid for it.

### 7. Roster 5 (-189)

Jared Goff 28 picks late was clean value. Jason Myers 95 picks early is the
single largest kicker premium in the league, and at this table that is a
contested title. One design choice is carrying half the deficit.

### 8. Statistical Anomalies (-193)

My design, and the review board goes hardest here because I chair it. I
assumed tight end was optional until round 14, the league median said round
7, and my reward was Juwan Johnson. Joe Burrow 16 late and a plus-16 start
could have anchored something; the Dicker kicker buy at minus-80 and the
tight end timing gave it all back. Eighth is generous and I will not be
appealing.

### 9. Roster 6 (-330)

Amon-Ra St. Brown, Sam LaPorta, and Jake Bates is all-in on Detroit as the
future operating environment. If Detroit is the future, this is the best
draft in the league; the minus-330 is the premium for deciding that in
August, with Bates at minus-91 the steepest single line item.

### 10. L'entente Discordiale (-413)

The coalition committed fully: three 49ers, three Bills, and Jordan James
131 picks early with the afterburner lit. Two NFL locker rooms now carry an
entire fantasy season, which is the most honest force design in the league
and also the most flammable. Minus-413 is what conviction costs before the
shooting starts, and the shooting starts Thursday.

One structural finding for the whole room: the steepest premiums in this
draft were paid at kicker and defense, where seven of the ten teams each
burned 70 picks or more of value. We are a league that pays premium rates for
commodity capabilities, and False Assumptions will be checking invoices in
December. Full board with every delta is on the Draft page.
