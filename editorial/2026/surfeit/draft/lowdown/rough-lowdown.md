# The Lowdown

## Every Draft Is a List of Assumptions

A draft is a planning document. Ten of us just published our assumptions
about the 2026 season, in writing, with timestamps, and the season's entire
job is to red-team them. Welcome to The Surfeit, where the wargame is real
and the assumptions are legally discoverable.

Three assumption classes showed up on draft night. First, concentration
bets: L'entente Discordiale now holds three 49ers and three Bills, which
means two NFL games a week decide most of that team's fate. Jordan James at
pick 123, against a consensus rank of 254, is the same bet with the
afterburner lit. Second, divestment bets: Roster 4 (name pending, like half
this league) drafted zero defenses in fifteen picks. Divest-to-invest is
real doctrine. This is divest-and-hope, and I respect the commitment.
Third, timing bets, and I go first because I made the dumbest one: I assumed
tight end was optional until round 14 while the league median said round 7,
and my reward was Juwan Johnson. Swifty Mahomey at least got paid for
waiting, landing the actual Patrick Mahomes at pick 114 after wearing his
name all August.

Still, assumption-heavy plans win wars all the time. The waiver wire is
industrial mobilization; half of these bets get bailed out, zero questions asked, by
October and we all pretend the plan worked.

The difference this year is that the assumptions are being tracked. False
Assumptions will read them back later, verbatim, with dates. Mine included.
Consider this your only warning.

The shooting starts Thursday. Hardware and capsules below; best of luck to
all ten of you, and slightly less to whoever draws me in Week 1.
