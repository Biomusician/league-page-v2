## Statistical Anomalies vs L'entente Discordiale

Two acquisition strategies walk into Week 1. Statistical Anomalies ran the program of record: buy the requirement, ignore the shiny objects, and let the milestone reviews sort it out. L'entente Discordiale ran concurrency: commit to three lots of San Francisco, three lots of Buffalo, and start production before the test reports come back.

Since I grade my own team first, let's start with the audit findings. I drafted one tight end, in round 14, and it was Juwan Johnson. The league median for a first TE was round 7; I showed up a full seven rounds after everyone else and left with the floor model. I also took MarShawn Lloyd 45 picks ahead of where FantasyPros' consensus board had him, and a kicker in round 13 that the same board had 80 spots later. The Burrow-to-Chase stack is the flagship here, and Burrow at pick 66 was 16 spots below sticker, so the checkbook wasn't all bad. Still, a TE room this thin would make any program office sweat.

Now the Entente. Josh Allen at pick 38 was 11 spots of value, Mike Evans at 78 was 18 more, and CMC at 1.03 needs no justification. Then it gets interesting: Dalton Kincaid went 48 picks early, the Bills DEF went 130 picks early, and Jordan James went at pick 123 against a consensus rank of 254. That is 131 spots of pure conviction. Combined with the CMC, Evans, and James block, this roster now holds three 49ers and three Bills, which means two NFL games a week decide most of this team's fate. Concurrency works great until one production line slips.

Week 1 verdict: my starters are the safer buy, their ceiling is the better brochure. Fight's On.
