<b>Justin Herbert, QB — Gary — LAC vs. ARI</b>

There are easier ways to begin a fantasy season than asking Justin Herbert to throw against a secondary that finished 24th in passing yards allowed. RotoBaller has Herbert as a strong QB1 and points to the combination of Mike McDaniel taking over the offense and a healthy offensive line as reasons to expect another step forward. FantasyPros' current Week 1 Superflex consensus has Herbert all the way up at QB5, while FanDuel notes Arizona surrendered 230.8 passing yards per game last season. This isn't a streamer getting promoted by matchup; it's a good quarterback entering Week 1 with both the offensive environment and opponent pushing in the same direction.

<b>The play:</b> Strong QB1. Arizona provides a good first test of whether the McDaniel-Herbert pairing is going to produce the fantasy jump everyone spent August pricing in.

<b>Bucky Irving, RB — MJ — TB at CIN</b>

Cincinnati's 2025 run defense was less a defensive unit than an invitation. FanDuel has the Bengals dead last in rushing defense at 147.1 yards allowed per game, while RotoBaller notes they surrendered the most fantasy points and rushing yards to opposing backs. That outlet calls Irving a strong RB2 even with Kenny Gainwell around, and FantasyPros experts overwhelmingly preferred Irving in a current Week 1 comparison against Christian Watson. Tampa Bay does not need to invent volume here. The opponent should give Irving enough opportunity on its own.

<b>The play:</b> RB2 with legitimate upside. If Cincinnati's front looks anything like last year's version, Irving could spend a lot of Sunday making the offseason timeshare discussion feel unnecessarily complicated.

<b>Kyle Pitts, TE — SHACtin' a Fool 🐻 (SHAC) — ATL at PIT</b>

This would normally be the point where everyone remembers the previous four years of Kyle Pitts discourse and reaches for the eject handle. The difference this time is that the breakout already happened. Pitts finished 2025 as the PPR TE2 with career highs in receptions and touchdowns, and Pittsburgh allowed the second-most fantasy points to opposing tight ends. FantasyPros currently has him TE5 for Week 1, while FanDuel notes the Steelers finished 29th in pass defense overall. The player, role and matchup are all finally telling the same story.

<b>The play:</b> Clear TE1. This is the rare Kyle Pitts recommendation based on what he has already done rather than what we have spent years hoping he might eventually do.

<b>David Montgomery, RB — Swifty Mahomey (PAPPIE) — HOU vs. BUF</b>

Montgomery gets one of the cleanest Week 1 volume/matchup combinations outside the elite tier. Buffalo finished 28th in rushing defense at 136.2 yards allowed per game, and RotoBaller specifically identifies Montgomery as a Week 1 matchup it loves after the Bills surrendered the sixth-most fantasy points to running backs in 2025. FantasyPros has him around RB20 in half-PPR, which is useful context: this is not an obvious top-five start, but rather a player whose weekly circumstances look better than the generic ranking. Houston may rotate Woody Marks, but Montgomery is expected to lead the backfield.

<b>The play:</b> Strong RB2. Buffalo's run defense gives Montgomery a realistic route to beating the fairly modest consensus ranking.

<b>Jayden Daniels, QB — Wild SeeKats (Seebass/Kats) — WAS at PHI</b>

Philadelphia is a good defense, which is exactly why Daniels is interesting here. The Eagles finished eighth against the pass, and FanDuel's projection is relatively restrained at 15.5 fantasy points. The counter is the one matchup trait that matters enormously for Daniels: Philadelphia allowed the second-most quarterback rushing yards <i>and</i> rushing touchdowns last season. RotoBaller still grades him as a solid QB1, while FantasyPros has him QB6 in its Week 1 Superflex consensus. A difficult conventional quarterback matchup becomes considerably less conventional when the quarterback is part running back.

<b>The play:</b> QB1. The passing matchup may suppress his ceiling somewhat, but Philadelphia's 2025 vulnerability to mobile quarterbacks gives Daniels a very different escape route.
