<b>DK Metcalf, WR — L'entente Discordiale (Borat/Jack) — PIT vs. ATL</b>

Metcalf is expected to play after entering Week 1 with an injury issue, but "available" and "good fantasy setup" are different questions. RotoBaller expects possible shadow coverage from A.J. Terrell and drops Metcalf to lower-end WR3 territory. FanDuel projects just 49.5 receiving yards and 9.4 PPR points. FantasyPros is somewhat more optimistic and calls him a strong FLEX now that his health has cleared, so this is not a unanimous bench call. It is a warning against treating the name on the roster as an automatic WR2.

<b>The fade:</b> FLEX, not set-and-forget WR2. The combination of recent injury and Terrell makes the downside considerably more credible than Metcalf's name value suggests.

<b>Jeremiyah Love, RB — Statistical Anomalies (McLovin) — ARI at LAC</b>

There are several independent ways this goes wrong. Love is coming back from a preseason high-ankle sprain, FantasyPros reported him as roughly 50/50 for Week 1 earlier this week, and even if active he gets a Chargers defense that allowed the fourth-fewest fantasy points to running backs last season. RotoBaller still sees RB2 value if he suits up, but explicitly recommends tempering expectations in his NFL debut. FantasyPros' own Derek Brown is below consensus because the ankle clouds both availability and early-season effectiveness. That's enough compounding uncertainty to avoid betting aggressively on his first professional game.

<b>The fade:</b> Sit if there is a credible alternative. If he is active, he is still playable, but "rookie debut + high ankle sprain + top-tier RB defense" is an impressive collection of reasons to make someone else prove it first.

<b>Rashee Rice, WR — Swanson — KC vs. DEN</b>

Rice is probably still going into many lineups, but this is a week to suppress expectations. Denver finished seventh against the pass at just 187.2 yards allowed per game, and Rice managed only 9.8 PPR points when these teams met last season. RotoBaller calls him a low-end WR2 and specifically points to Patrick Surtain II as the problem. FanDuel lands in almost exactly the same neighborhood with a 12.0 PPR projection and only 68 receiving yards. The volume floor remains real; the ceiling is what Denver threatens.

<b>The fade:</b> Downgrade to WR2/FLEX expectations. Start him if that's what the roster requires, but this is a poor week to expect the high-end Rice outcome.

<b>Terry McLaurin, WR — Los Bandidos (Bandit) — WAS at PHI</b>

McLaurin gets essentially the opposite half of the Jayden Daniels argument. Daniels can escape Philadelphia's pass defense with his legs; McLaurin has to deal with the secondary directly. RotoBaller expects a matchup with Quinyon Mitchell and calls McLaurin risky. FanDuel projects just 49.2 receiving yards, 0.24 touchdowns and 8.2 PPR points against an Eagles defense that finished eighth in passing yards allowed. There is always one-play touchdown upside with McLaurin, but the median outcome looks much more like a FLEX than a comfortable WR2.

<b>The fade:</b> FLEX territory. The talent stays in the lineup discussion; the matchup argues strongly against assuming normal production.

<b>Stefon Diggs, WR — Dave — WAS at PHI</b>

Same Philadelphia problem, different corner. RotoBaller expects Diggs to spend significant time dealing with Cooper DeJean and puts both Washington receivers in its difficult-matchup bucket. The current FantasyPros consensus is even colder: Diggs sits around WR50 for Week 1 and FLEX110 in half-PPR, with an enormous expert range that tells you how little confidence there is in his first game with Washington. The season-long case for Diggs is still defensible—his underlying 2025 efficiency remained good—but this is an awful week to use that long-term argument as a reason to force him into the lineup.

<b>The fade:</b> Bench unless lineup depth forces the issue. Between a new offense, target competition and Philadelphia's secondary, Week 1 is better used to learn what his Washington role actually is.
